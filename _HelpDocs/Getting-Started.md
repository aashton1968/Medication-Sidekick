This guide helps you get up and running quickly with Medication Sidekick.

## Quick Steps

1. Open the app and review the Home screen.
2. Add your first medication: name, type, and dosage.
3. Set its schedule — how often, and which times of day it's linked to.
4. Set its quantities — how much you have, and how much counts as one dose.
5. Check the Home screen's dose list to make sure today's doses look correct.

## Home Screen

![Image](MS_IPH_HomeView)

The Home screen shows your next dose, progress, and today's doses at a glance.

## Entering a Medication

Add a medication from the Medications tab (the **+** button). Everything below is on one screen — name and dosage, schedule, and quantities are all part of the same form when you add a medication, and the same three groups reappear separately whenever you edit one later.

### Name, dosage, and type

![Image](MS_IPH_MedicationEditView)

Enter the medication's name and dosage (e.g. "500 mg"), any instructions, and pick its type (tablet, capsule, liquid, injection, and so on) so it gets the right icon.

### Linking the schedule

![Image](MS_IPH_MedicationScheduleEditView)

Choose how often it's taken — daily, specific days, or as needed — then pick which **Schedule Times** it's linked to (Morning, Evening, and so on; manage these from the More tab). A dose time defaults to its schedule time and keeps following it if you ever change that schedule time later — unless you override the time for this medication specifically, in which case it sticks to the time you set.

### Setting quantities

![Image](MS_IPH_MedicationStockView)

Enter how much you currently have on hand, how many units count as a single dose, and the unit (tablets, mL, and so on). The app uses this to track stock as doses are taken and to warn you before you run out.

## More Tab

![Image](MS_IPH_MoreView)

Open the More tab for Help, Schedule Times, Settings, and About.

## Tip

After your first setup, check the Home screen once in the morning and once in the evening to keep your schedule accurate.
