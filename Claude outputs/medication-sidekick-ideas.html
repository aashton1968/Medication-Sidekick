New Features

8 ideas

Capabilities the app doesn't have yet — each one addresses a gap visible in the competitive landscape or extends a natural hook in your existing architecture.

iOS Home Screen & Lock Screen Widgets

Feature High
The single biggest visibility gap versus competitors. A small widget showing the next upcoming dose (medication name, time, meal slot) and a medium widget showing today's adherence ring or dose checklist would keep users engaged without opening the app. Your TodaySnapshotSection and NextDoseCard already compute everything the widget needs — it's a presentation layer on top of existing logic. Consider a Lock Screen widget too (the compact circular format showing doses remaining today). WidgetKit with SwiftData AppIntentTimelineProvider is the path; the widget reads from the shared app group container.
⏱ 2–3 days 📂 New WidgetKit target + shared App Group 💡 Medisafe, EveryDose, Round Health all lead with widgets

Refill Reminders & Pharmacy Awareness

Feature High
You already track currentStock, doseQuantity, and compute daysOfSupply — but the user only sees this passively on the detail view. Turn it active: schedule a local notification when any medication drops below a configurable threshold (e.g. 7 days of supply). Add an optional "refill" action on the notification itself that lets them log a restock amount without opening the app. A "Refill History" list on the medication detail view (a new lightweight model tracking date + quantity added) would give users a record they can show their pharmacy. This is the most natural extension of your existing stock system.
⏱ 2–3 days 📂 New RefillEvent model + notification category 💡 MyMeds and CareZone made refills a core feature

Apple Watch Companion

Feature Medium
A watch app doesn't need to replicate the full UI — just three things: a complication showing the next dose, a notification action to mark as taken from the wrist, and a simple "Today" view listing pending doses with tap-to-take. Your MedicationDose.markAsTaken() already handles stock deduction. WatchConnectivity or a shared SwiftData store via App Group handles sync. EveryDose lists smartwatch support as a differentiator; most competitors don't have it. This is a premium-tier feature that justifies the Pro subscription for many users.
⏱ 4–5 days 📂 New WatchKit target 💡 Strong Pro-tier differentiator

Caregiver Sharing ("Medfriend")

Feature Medium
Let users designate a family member or caregiver who receives a push notification when a dose is missed past the grace period. This is Medisafe's signature feature ("Medfriend") and one of the most requested capabilities in the medication app space. Implementation: a CloudKit shared zone or a lightweight server-side notification relay. Start simple — a single shared link that grants read-only access to adherence data for today, no login required (a signed URL with an expiry). A caregiver doesn't need the full app, just a confirmation that their person took their meds.
⏱ 5–7 days 📂 CloudKit sharing or lightweight backend 💡 Medisafe's #1 cited differentiator in every review

Drug Interaction Checker

Feature Medium
When a user adds a new medication, check it against their existing medications for known interactions. The openFDA API (free, no key required for low-volume) provides drug interaction data. Display a clear warning card — not medical advice, but an "ask your pharmacist about" prompt. Apple Health's built-in Medications feature includes US-only interaction checking, so this would be especially valuable for your international users. Keep the disclaimer prominent: "This is not a substitute for professional medical advice."
⏱ 3–4 days 📂 New InteractionService + openFDA integration 💡 Medisafe's interaction checker is frequently cited as a reason to choose it

Adherence Reports for Doctor Visits

Feature Medium
Generate a clean PDF or shareable summary showing adherence data over a configurable period (7/14/30/90 days). Your MedicationAdherenceService.rollingSummary() already computes exactly the data needed — per-medication taken/missed/skipped counts and adherence percentages. The output: a one-page PDF with a header (patient name, date range), per-medication adherence bars, a list of any missed doses with dates, and the stock status. MyTherapy's "doctor-ready reports" are their biggest differentiator and they're completely free — users print them or email them before appointments. This could be a Pro feature.
⏱ 2–3 days 📂 New ReportGenerator using existing AdherenceService 💡 MyTherapy's most praised feature across all reviews

HealthKit Integration

Feature Low
Write medication dose events to HealthKit so they appear alongside Apple Health's built-in Medications view. This makes Medication Sidekick a better citizen of the iOS health ecosystem and appeals to users who want a single health record. Read access could also pull in medications the user already logged in Apple Health to seed their medication list during onboarding. The integration is straightforward: HKClinicalType.medicationRecord for reading, HKCategorySample for writing dose events.
⏱ 2–3 days 📂 HealthKit entitlement + new HealthKitSyncService

Injection Site Rotation Tracker

Feature Low
For users with medicationType == .injection, add a body map that tracks where they last injected and suggests the next site in rotation. This is a niche feature but a powerful one — DoneDose built their entire brand around it. Your existing MedicationType enum already identifies injection medications; a new InjectionSite model linked to MedicationDose would track the location. Show a simple front/back body outline with tappable zones (abdomen quadrants, thighs, upper arms). Insulin users, biologic medication users, and GLP-1 users would all benefit.
⏱ 3–4 days 📂 New InjectionSite model + body map view 💡 DoneDose's core differentiator

UX Improvements

6 ideas

Making the existing experience smoother, faster, or more delightful — changes that improve retention without requiring new data models.

One-Tap Dose Confirmation from Notifications

UX Quick Win High
Add UNNotificationAction buttons directly to medication reminders: "Take" and "Skip." When the user long-presses or swipes the notification, they can mark the dose without opening the app at all. Your notification IDs already use the meddose. prefix convention, so mapping the action back to the correct MedicationDose is straightforward. This is the single biggest friction reducer — every review of competing apps cites "one-tap confirmation" as the feature users value most. The markAsTaken() and markAsSkipped() methods handle the transitions.
⏱ 1 day 📂 MedicationNotificationService + AppDelegate handler 💡 Rated #1 most important feature across user surveys

Streaks & Gentle Gamification

UX High
Show a streak counter on the home view: "14 days — all doses taken." Your CongratsMessages.swift already exists for positive reinforcement — extend it to be streak-aware. A simple consecutive-days counter computed from the dose history (days where adherence = 100%) is enough. Add a subtle animation when the streak increments. Keep it non-punitive (consistent with your design philosophy): if a streak breaks, the messaging should be encouraging ("Let's start a new one") rather than guilt-inducing. MyTherapy and Mango Health both use streaks; it's proven to increase retention. Consider a small achievement system: "First week," "30-day streak," "100 doses taken."
⏱ 1–2 days 📂 New StreakService + HomeView integration 💡 Aligns with your non-punitive design philosophy

Better Onboarding Flow

UX Medium
Replace the cold start (seed data + paywall prompt) with a 3-step welcome: (1) "What medications do you take?" — a quick-add flow with autocomplete from a bundled drug name database, (2) "When do you usually take them?" — set meal times to match their real schedule, (3) "Enable reminders" — notification permission in context. Remove the seeded sample medications (Metformin, Lisinopril, Atorvastatin) for new users — starting with real data is more engaging than a demo. The paywall can appear after they've added their first medication and seen their first reminder, when the value is concrete.
⏱ 2–3 days 📂 New OnboardingView + modified AppStartupSequence

Swipe Actions on Today View

UX Quick Win Medium
Add swipe gestures to dose rows in TodayView: swipe right to mark as taken (green), swipe left to skip (grey). Currently users need to tap into the dose to change its status. Swipe-to-act is the standard iOS pattern for list items and dramatically reduces the number of taps to complete a daily check-in. The TodayView already renders dose rows with status — this is purely a gesture layer on top of existing markAsTaken() / markAsSkipped() calls.
⏱ 0.5 days 📂 TodayView swipeActions modifier

Liquid Glass / iOS 26 Visual Refresh

UX Medium
You've already adopted Liquid Glass for Diabetic Sidekick — bring the same treatment to Medication Sidekick. The app currently uses a custom theme system (ThemeProtocol with Main()) that could incorporate the glass material and updated navigation style. This gives the app a fresh, modern feel and signals active maintenance to App Store browsers. Focus on the home view, today view, and navigation chrome first. Your semantic color tokens (surfaceElevated, bgBase) map naturally to the glass material tiers.
⏱ 2–3 days 📂 ThemeManager + view updates 💡 Already done for Diabetic Sidekick

Medication Photos

UX Low
Let users attach a photo to each medication — either from camera or photo library. This helps with visual identification ("is this the round white one or the oval blue one?"), which is especially valuable for users managing many medications. Store as a resized JPEG in the app's documents directory with the filename keyed to the medication ID. Show the thumbnail on the medication list, detail view, and in Today's dose rows. Caring Village and Pillboxie both emphasize visual identification as a safety feature.
⏱ 1–2 days 📂 Medication model extension + PhotosPicker

Growth & Monetization

5 ideas

Getting more users, improving conversion, and making the Pro tier more compelling.

Restructure Pro Tier Around High-Value Features

Growth High
The current free-tier gate (5 medications) is functional but doesn't showcase what Pro offers beyond more slots. Consider making the free tier unlimited medications but gating higher-value features behind Pro: adherence reports, caregiver sharing, Apple Watch, refill reminders, advanced analytics, and export. This follows the Medisafe model — everyone gets the core reminder engine for free, and power users who want insights and sharing pay. It widens the funnel: a user with 3 medications never hits the current gate, so they never see the value of upgrading. With feature-gated Pro, they see locked features every day and understand what they're missing.
⏱ 1–2 days to restructure gates 📂 SubscriptionService + feature flag checks 💡 Most successful medication apps use feature-gated, not count-gated, tiers

App Store Optimization

Growth Quick Win High
Your IPA screenshots (visible in your asset catalog) are already solid, but the App Store listing itself can be tuned. Key actions: use all 30 keyword characters (target "pill reminder," "medication tracker," "drug interaction," "refill reminder," "dose tracker"), write the subtitle to lead with the primary use case ("Track Medications · Reminders · Stock"), and make the first three screenshots tell a complete story (Home → Today → Adherence chart). Localize the listing into French (for Quebec) and other high-value markets. Request ratings via SKStoreReviewController after a user hits a 7-day streak — they're at peak satisfaction.
⏱ 1 day 📂 App Store Connect + in-app review prompt

Improved Paywall with Social Proof

Growth Medium
Your current PaywallView has a clean design (red diagonal header, black pricing card). Enhance it with: a personalized stat ("You've tracked 47 doses this month — Pro unlocks insights into your adherence patterns"), a before/after comparison showing free vs Pro features side by side, and a "Try free for 7 days" trial offer if you're not already using one. The trial should auto-start when they tap Subscribe — Apple handles the billing. Adding the user's own data to the paywall (how many doses tracked, their streak length) personalizes the pitch. Position Pro as a companion to their existing behavior, not a gate.
⏱ 1–2 days 📂 PaywallView + SubscriptionService

Family / Household Plan

Growth Low
Many medication apps are used by caregivers managing medications for family members. Adding separate profiles within the app (each with their own medication list, schedules, and adherence data) would serve this audience. A household Pro plan (slightly higher price, up to 5 profiles) becomes a natural upsell. EveryDose offers up to 5 family profiles; CareZone was built entirely around this use case. Start simple: a profile switcher in the sidebar, each profile backed by a separate SwiftData store or filtered by a profileID field on existing models.
⏱ 4–5 days 📂 New Profile model + data partitioning

Cross-Promote the Sidekick Family

Growth Quick Win Low
You have four Sidekick apps — users of one are natural candidates for the others. Add a small "More from Sidekick" section in Settings or About that links to Diabetic Sidekick, Memory Sidekick, and Photos Sidekick in the App Store. Use SKStoreProductViewController so they can see the listing without leaving your app. This is zero development effort for cross-pollination across your app portfolio. Consider a "Sidekick Bundle" on the App Store if your subscription structures allow it.
⏱ 0.5 days 📂 SettingsView or AboutView
